# Prompt: Generate a Senior UX Case Study for Skoot Ride

Create a polished, recruiter-ready UX case study for **Skoot Ride**, a smart electric mobility platform that enables riders to lease electric tuk-tuks and electric bikes, making vehicle ownership accessible so people can earn a sustainable income without the high upfront cost of purchasing a vehicle.

The tone should reflect the perspective of a Senior Product Designer explaining the design thinking, user research, UX decisions, trade-offs, and business outcomes—not marketing copy.

The case study should feel similar to portfolios from designers at Uber, Lime, Bolt, Airbnb, Stripe, or Google: editorial, visual, insight-driven, and focused on solving meaningful user problems.

---

# Project Overview

Write an engaging overview introducing Skoot Ride.

Include:

* Skoot Ride is an electric mobility leasing platform.
* It connects aspiring riders and delivery partners with electric tuk-tuks and electric bikes through affordable lease plans.
* The platform removes the financial barrier of purchasing a vehicle outright.
* Instead of buying a vehicle, users lease one and immediately begin generating income.
* Explain how the experience balances trust, affordability, operational transparency, and ease of use throughout the leasing journey.

Mention that as Product Designer I designed the end-to-end experience across:

* Lease Selection
* Vehicle Discovery
* Identity Verification (KYC)
* Lease Approval Dashboard
* Vehicle Management
* Charging Experience
* Wallet & Payments

# Writing Style

* Write from the perspective of a Senior Product Designer.
* Explain **why** every design decision was made—not just what was designed.
* Focus on user behavior, UX reasoning, business goals, and measurable outcomes.
* Avoid marketing language.
* Keep the writing concise, thoughtful, and professional.
* Make the case study suitable for a portfolio reviewed by hiring managers at leading product companies.
* Clearly label all metrics as **estimated outcomes based on UX best practices** unless they are supported by actual usability testing or product analytics. This keeps the case study credible while demonstrating the intended impact of the design decisions.
